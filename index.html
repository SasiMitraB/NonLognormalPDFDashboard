Supplementary Material · Behara & Seta 2026

Non-lognormal density probability distribution and turbulent Mach numbers in the local Interstellar Medium from three-dimensional data

The probability distribution function (PDF) of gas density is a key tool used to study turbulence and star formation in the interstellar medium (ISM), and is typically modelled as a lognormal distribution. Observational studies have been limited to two-dimensional column-density maps, which fail to capture the intermittency, which are key to alternative, non-lognormal models such as the Castaing-Hopkins Model. We use the parsec-scale three-dimensional dust map of the local ISM ($\le 1250 \, \mathrm{pc}$) from Edenhofer et al. (2024) to measure, for the first time, the volumetric density PDF of individual molecular clouds directly in 3D.

We identify $766$ clouds using the dendrogram algorithm, study their turbulent envelopes, and fit each cloud's density PDF with four candidate models: Lognormal, Castaing-Hopkins, and their modifications with a power-law tail. Of the $475$ clouds successfully fit ($62\%$), the population shows an overwhelming preference for Castaing-Hopkins models ($76\%$ of fitted clouds, compared to $24\%$ for lognormal models), with a substantial fraction requiring non-zero intermittency. We report this as the first observational evidence for non-lognormal density PDFs in the ISM, and find the local ISM is dominated by transonic to mildly supersonic turbulence.
